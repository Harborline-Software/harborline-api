# Adversarial Review Round 3 — The Manager

**Date:** 2026-06-06
**Perspective:** The Manager — effort calibration and production readiness
**Scope:** Full catalog (168 entries) + spec set (660+ contract files, 265 Semantic + companions)
**Prior rounds read:** Yes — Round 1 hardening log (88 findings, 48 fixed-in-batch-31), Round 2 hardening log (29 findings, 20 fixed-in-batch-32), UPF Stage 2 meta-validation, original Round 1 Manager findings file

---

## Summary

The corpus is no closer to M1-shippable than it was at the end of Round 2. Round 1's three High-severity Manager findings — TextBox/NumericTextBox under-spec (MG-1, MG-2) and 231+ files of metadata rot (MG-3) — were all logged "Fix-deferred M2" and **none have been touched** between Round 1 (2026-06-05) and now. Meanwhile Round 1 and Round 2 each added two-dozen-plus High/Medium findings whose dispositions live ONLY in the hardening logs, never propagated into the contract files themselves; an implementor opening a contract for an `app-priority: critical, library-scope: v1` component sees `Accepted-risk M1` against gaps that the logs internally classify as production-blocking. Worst of all, the original Round 1 inversion (future-wave specs out-detail the critical/v1 baseline) is now larger, not smaller — a single deferred 4-contract Sankey chart spec (250 lines) is denser than the 4-contract DropDownList (212 lines) which is on the M0 Top-20 critical-path. The corpus also contains an estimated **100+ ghost spec files** (no catalog counterpart), including 4 explicit "do not build" domain-tier components and 7 components that have been authored twice under different categories. The Phase 6 catalog-promotion verdict of PASS-WITH-NOTES is, on closer inspection, papering over a corpus that is closer to "tractable M1 build" than "ready for M1 build" — but the gap should be re-stated honestly, not deferred again.

## M2 Debt Register Estimate

A fair accounting of "what does the team owe before M1 actually ships" — not just "what's in the log."

### Logged deferred items (from Round 1 + Round 2 + UPF logs)
Round 1 hardening log: **24** items labeled `Fix-deferred M2`
Round 2 hardening log: **9** items labeled DEFERRED / DEFERRED-tracking / ACCEPTED
UPF meta-validation: **9** items in the "Deferred Work Register"
**Logged total: 42 unique items** (some overlap between Round 1 and UPF)

### Contract-embedded items (what the corpus carries inside files)
`Accepted-risk M1` markers in `.Semantic.md`/`.Interaction.md`/`.Accessibility.md`/`.Styling.md` files: **552 occurrences across 240 distinct files**
`Fix-deferred M2` markers actually inside contract files: **1** (Scheduler.Interaction.md only)
`Fix-in-M1` markers: **63 occurrences**

This gap between "552 Accepted-risk markers" and "1 Fix-deferred marker" is exactly MG3-3 — the contracts do not surface deferred work, they surface accepted work. From the contract reader's perspective, the corpus has 552 accepted gaps, not 24 deferred ones. That is a much worse story than the hardening log tells.

### Is M2 tractable?

**No, not as currently scoped.** The Round 1+2 logs claim 42 deferred items but the contracts themselves carry 240 files with 552 "we'll fix this later" notes. A realistic M2 sprint to clear the embedded debt would touch ~36% of all contract files (240/660). That is not an M2 — that is an M1.5 stabilization wave, which the plan does not currently budget for.

**Honest re-scope:**
- The "M2 debt register" is closer to 200-250 items once contract-embedded markers are counted, not 42.
- Of those 200-250, perhaps 30-40 are genuinely production-blocking on `library-scope: v1` components (WCAG hard-failures on shipping primitives like MultiSelect/DatePicker/Switch/Tooltip — see MG3-4).
- The remaining 160-220 are forward-spec or accepted-risk for future-wave components, which is fine but should be re-labeled to distinguish "M1 ships this way" from "M1 won't ship this until M2."

**Realistic Manager recommendation:** before declaring M1 ready, run a Sonnet sweep that does three things in one pass:
1. Fix MG3-2 (metadata rot, ~236 files);
2. Propagate hardening-log dispositions into the contracts (MG3-3, ~50-100 line edits);
3. Reclassify `Accepted-risk M1` on `library-scope: v1` High-severity WCAG failures as `Blocking-before-v1-ship` (MG3-4, ~10 files).

That is ~1 hour of Sonnet subagent time and would convert the corpus from "specs ready in name only" to "specs ready in fact." Anything less and the M1 implementation wave will spend its first sprint re-discovering the deferred work that the spec corpus exists to surface.
